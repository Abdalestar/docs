NFC tags and QR codes do the same job in Qtap. A customer brings their phone to your loyalty program and earns a stamp, earns points, or records a check-in. What changes is how the phone connects, and that affects who can use each one and what it costs you.

## How each one works

An NFC tag is a small physical tag you stick on a counter or a table. A customer opens the Qtap app and holds their phone close to it. The app reads the tag and runs the action you set for that tag.

A QR code is an image you generate in the dashboard and then print or show on a screen. A customer points their phone camera at it, and a link opens that runs the same kind of action.

Both can add a stamp, award points, or record a check-in. You choose the action per tag or per code.

## Who can use them

Earning from a tap needs the Qtap app. The tap has to carry the customer's account with it, so no stamp lands and no points move unless the app is the one reading the tag.

The customer still sees something. Each tag holds a web address, and a phone that cannot pass that address to the Qtap app opens it in a browser instead. The page reads **You tapped a Qtap tag**, tells the customer to open the app and tap again, and offers a **Get the Qtap app** button. The page writes nothing back to Qtap, so the tap never reaches the tag's Taps count either.

<Frame caption="What a phone without the Qtap app shows after a tap. Every tag shows this same page.">
  <img src="/images/merchants/nfc-tags/nfc-tap-no-app.png" alt="The Qtap tag page on a phone, showing the You tapped a Qtap tag heading and a Get the Qtap app button" />
</Frame>

The page names nothing else on purpose. It never shows your business or the reward the customer was working toward, so someone standing at your counter learns only that they need the app. Expect your staff to be asked about it, and have them point the customer at a QR code for that visit.

A QR code is more forgiving, because any phone camera can open it. A scan from a plain camera opens a join page: a new customer fills in a phone number, becomes a member, and earns from that first scan, no app needed. A customer who is already a member is welcomed back without earning again; their repeat earning happens through a staff scan at the counter or from inside the Qtap app.

## What they cost

NFC tags are physical items you buy. Order them under **Settings → Billing** at $19 per tag, then register each one by entering its serial number.

QR codes are free to generate in the dashboard and count against your plan's monthly allowance. You can make a code reusable, make it one-time so it stops working after a single scan, or generate a batch at once. You can also set an expiry date or a scan limit on a code.

<Note>
A tap and a scan run the same earning rules, so a points-multiplier campaign like double points on Tuesdays reaches your tap customers too, and a completed card or a birthday reward lands either way. The rule that does treat them differently is the stamp cap: a tag adds at most one stamp per card per customer per day, while a QR code has no daily cap. See [How a tap works](/merchants/nfc-tags/tap-and-earn) for the detail.
</Note>

## Which one to choose

Reach for an NFC tag when your regulars already have the app and you want the quickest action at the counter. One tap and the stamp is in, and a physical tag feels premium next to the till.

Reach for a QR code when you want to reach anyone with a phone, print on a poster, a receipt, a table tent, or a shop window, or run a one-time or time-limited promotion. Plenty of merchants use both: a counter tag for regulars, and a printed code that first-time visitors can scan before they have the app.
