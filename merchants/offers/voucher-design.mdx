A voucher is the thing a customer looks at on their phone before they walk into your
shop. You choose the colour, the background photo, the badge in the corner and the
terms on the claim sheet. Qtap works out the rest so it reads clearly whatever you
pick. Everything here lives on the **Appearance** step of the offer form. For the
deal itself, see [Public offers](/merchants/offers/overview).

## Pick a colour, any colour

**Qtap default** gives you charcoal. **Your colour** opens a picker and a box for a
full hex code such as `#123A2E`. Type all six characters, or the form asks you for
"a full colour code, like #8E4A63".

Qtap measures how light or dark your colour is and sets the text from that. Dark
colours get white text with a gold accent. Pale colours get near-black text with a
charcoal accent. You never pick a text colour, so you cannot end up with a voucher
nobody can read.

## Put your own photo behind it

**Upload cover** takes a JPG, PNG or WebP up to 10MB. Aim for a landscape 16:9 photo
at 1200x675 pixels or larger. Anything else still works: Qtap centre-crops whatever
you give it and turns nothing away for being the wrong shape.

<Frame caption="The small cross removes the photo and puts you back to a flat colour.">
  <img src="/images/merchants/offers/voucher-design-cover-uploaded.png" alt="The cover image field showing an uploaded photo of coffee beans with a remove button" />
</Frame>

Qtap strips your photo down to its light and shade, then tints it with your card
colour. The photo gives the voucher texture and your colour still sets the mood. Put
a dark photo of coffee beans on one offer and a bright bakery counter on the next,
and both still read as your shop.

Some things worth knowing when you choose a picture:

- Keep the subject near the middle. The crop takes the centre.
- Well-lit photos survive the tint best. Very dark or very busy ones turn to mush.
- Skip pictures with writing in them. The tint washes over text, and your voucher
  title sits on top of it.

**Use my page cover** borrows the cover photo already on your merchant page, which
saves you a second upload when the same picture suits both.

<Note>
Qtap makes the cropped copy when you save the offer, so the preview before that
point shows your original upload. Both centre on the same spot, so what you see is
what customers get.
</Note>

## Write the corner tag and the terms

**Badge label** rewrites the small tag in the voucher corner, up to 14 characters.
Qtap prints it in capitals, and it takes Arabic as happily as English. Leave it empty
and Qtap names the reward type for you.

<Frame caption="The badge is your words, in the corner of the card.">
  <img src="/images/merchants/offers/voucher-design-badge.png" alt="The badge label field filled with ROASTERY PICK and the same text on the voucher preview" />
</Frame>

The **claim sheet** is what a customer reads just before they confirm a claim. You can
rewrite the advice line and add up to three terms of your own, such as dine-in only or
one per table. The line about how long the voucher lasts is always worked out from
your own dates and claim window, so you never have to keep it in step by hand.

## Check it before you publish

The preview on the right is the card itself, drawn the same way your customers see
it. Three buttons above it switch between how the voucher looks on your merchant
page, how it looks once a customer holds it, and how it looks after they use it.

<Frame caption="A claimed voucher, with the gold Use button a customer taps at the counter.">
  <img src="/images/merchants/offers/voucher-design-preview-states.png" alt="The live preview showing the In their wallet state of a voucher" />
</Frame>

<Warning>
A used, expired or fully claimed voucher drops your photo and turns plain. A used one
keeps your colour on the stub alone. That is deliberate, so a spent voucher never gets
mistaken for a live one at the counter.
</Warning>

The name and logo on the voucher come from your business details rather than this
form. To change those, edit your [merchant page](/merchants/settings/merchant-page).

Colour, cover, badge and claim sheet all stay editable after customers start
claiming, so you can redress a live offer without ending it. The deal itself locks on
the first claim.
