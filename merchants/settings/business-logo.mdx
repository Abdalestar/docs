You upload your logo once. Qtap then uses it on the loyalty card in your customer's app, on the
Wallet pass, on your public merchant page, on the join page a camera scan opens, and at the top of
the emails Qtap sends on your behalf.

Because those places are small, Qtap does not use your file as-is. It builds a few things from it
the moment you upload.

## Where to upload it

Go to **Settings** in the sidebar. The **Business Logo** card sits at the top of the page. Click
**Upload Logo**, pick a file, and it saves straight away. There is no separate save button for this
card.

<Frame caption="Settings opens on the Business Logo card. Upload Logo replaces the current file immediately, and the line under it is the size Qtap suggests.">
  <img src="/images/merchants/settings/business-logo-01-upload.png" alt="The Business Logo card in Settings, with the current logo, the Upload Logo button outlined, and the recommended size hint outlined below it" />
</Frame>

Settings is open to the owner only. If you are a manager, you can change the same logo from the
**Merchant Logo** field in the merchant page editor instead, where the limit is 10 MB and the file
must be a PNG or a JPG.

## What Qtap builds from your file

<Frame caption="One file in, and Qtap keeps a trimmed copy, a watermark, and a note about transparency.">
  <img src="/images/merchants/settings/business-logo-pipeline.svg" alt="Diagram: one uploaded image becomes a trimmed copy, a watermark and a transparency note, which feed the loyalty card, the Wallet pass, Qtap emails, the join page and the merchant page" />
</Frame>

**A trimmed copy.** Qtap finds the edge of your artwork and cuts the empty space around it, then
sizes the result down to 512 pixels. Padding in your file costs you nothing. The bakery logo in the
screenshots below arrived as a 1024 pixel square whose artwork only fills the middle 658 pixels, so
more than a third of it was blank, and the version customers see has none of that blank space left.

A tall or wide logo is kept whole rather than cropped square. The coffee shop logo further down
comes out 466 by 512, with nothing cut off.

**A watermark.** A faint, oversized shape of your logo that sits behind the artwork on a loyalty
card. It is drawn at about six percent opacity, so you should have to look for it.

**A note about transparency**, which tells the app how to treat the watermark without having to
work it out on a customer's phone.

You can see the first two in the pass design studio, which keeps each one on its own panel.

<Frame caption="The pass design studio on a points programme. The trimmed logo (1) and the watermark Qtap built from it (2). The watermark panel looks almost empty because a pale shape on a pale panel is hard to make out.">
  <img src="/images/merchants/settings/business-logo-02-derived.png" alt="The Logo and Watermark logo panels in the pass design studio, each showing a small preview of the asset Qtap derived" />
</Frame>

## Upload a PNG with a see-through background

This is the one choice that changes how your logo looks everywhere else.

A PNG with a see-through background sits directly on the card, and its watermark comes out as a
clean shape. A JPG, or a PNG that has a white or coloured square baked into it, keeps that square.
Qtap trims the square down to the artwork, but it cannot remove what is painted into the file, so
the block of colour travels with your logo onto every card and pass.

The two accounts in these screenshots show both outcomes. The bakery uploaded a file with a cream
background, and the cream tile follows its logo around:

<Frame caption="The coffee shop uploaded a PNG with a see-through background, so the cup sits straight on the panel with no tile behind it. Compare the cream tile in the screenshot above.">
  <img src="/images/merchants/settings/business-logo-06-derived-transparent.png" alt="The same Logo and Watermark logo panels on a stamp card account, where the logo has no background tile behind it" />
</Frame>

Square is a good habit because most of the places your logo appears are round or square, but it is
not a rule.

## Where it turns up

<Frame caption="The customer's card as staff see it at the counter. The logo sits in the panel on the right, and the watermark is the faint ring behind the card name.">
  <img src="/images/merchants/settings/business-logo-05-on-card.png" alt="A member's points card preview showing the merchant logo in the side panel and a faint watermark behind the card artwork" />
</Frame>

<Frame caption="The same logo at the top of the Wallet pass, in both the plain and the branded preview.">
  <img src="/images/merchants/settings/business-logo-03-wallet-pass.png" alt="Two Wallet pass previews side by side, each with the merchant logo outlined in the pass header" />
</Frame>

It also appears on the join page a customer reaches by scanning your QR code with a plain camera,
and in eight of the emails Qtap sends for you: the staff invitation, the weekly insights digest,
and six about your trial and billing. With no logo saved, those emails fall back to a circle with
your initials in it.

<Note>
On the join page, a branch that has its own **Location Logo** uses that instead of your business
logo. Check Settings and then Locations if a scan shows a logo you did not expect.
</Note>

## The second logo field is a different thing

The merchant page editor has two logo slots, and they do different jobs. **Merchant Logo** is the
one described above. **Merchant Logo (Transparent PNG)** is a separate file you upload by hand, and
it is used as the stamp icon on mobile loyalty cards. Qtap does not build anything from it.

<Frame caption="Merchant Logo (1) is your business logo. Merchant Logo (Transparent PNG) (2) is the stamp icon, and it stays empty until you set one.">
  <img src="/images/merchants/settings/business-logo-04-two-slots.png" alt="The Merchant Logo and Merchant Logo Transparent PNG fields in the merchant page editor, each outlined" />
</Frame>

<Warning>
The trimmed copy and the watermark are rebuilt in the background right after an upload, and nothing
retries if that fails. If your new logo shows on the Settings page but an old one is still on the
card previews, upload the file again.
</Warning>

<Tip>
In the merchant page editor the phone preview drops the watermark as soon as you pick a new logo,
and shows it again once you press **Update Merchant**. A blank watermark mid-edit is normal.
</Tip>

If you want a different watermark from the one Qtap built, the pass design studio lets you upload
your own and put the original back with a revert button.
