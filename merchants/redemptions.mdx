The Redemptions page is where your staff go when a customer wants to claim a reward. Open it from the sidebar at any time. It has two tabs: **Redeem** for processing rewards right now, and **History** for reviewing what's been processed.

<Frame caption="The Redeem tab: type the customer's code (1), search for them by name (2), or scan (3).">
  <img src="/images/redemptions/redeem-overview.png" alt="The Redemptions page on the Redeem tab, showing the Enter Code, Look Up Customer and Scan buttons" />
</Frame>

<Info>
The Members section also has a Redemptions page, but that one shows the redemption history for a single customer. This page handles the actual processing workflow. It's the tool staff use at the counter.
</Info>

## Two ways to redeem

<Frame caption="Both paths lead to the same place. Code entry is faster when the customer has their phone. Lookup works when they don't.">
  <img src="/images/redemptions/redemption-flow.svg" alt="Flow diagram showing the two redemption paths: entering a code and looking up a customer" />
</Frame>

### Enter a code

When a customer claims a reward, Qtap gives them an **8-digit code** in the app. It is digits only, so staff can type it on a numeric keypad without switching keyboards. Entering the code takes about 10 seconds at the counter.

<Frame caption="Type the 8-digit code from the customer's phone (1), then click Look Up (2).">
  <img src="/images/redemptions/enter-code.png" alt="The Enter Redemption Code card reading Enter the 8-digit code shown on the customer's phone, with a code typed in and the Look Up button beside it" />
</Frame>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Redemptions page">
    Click Redemptions in the sidebar. The Redeem tab opens by default.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select Enter Code">
    Click the **Enter Code** button if it is not already active.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Type the code">
    Enter the 8-digit code from the customer's phone. Press Enter or click **Look Up**. The button stays greyed out until you have typed at least six characters.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the reward card">
    The card shows the customer's name, the reward, which program it comes from, when it was earned, and the expiry date if one is set.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Confirm Redemption">
    The button appears only when the reward is still available. If it has already been used, the card says so instead.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Frame caption="A stamp card reward loaded from its code. The badge reads Ready, so Confirm Redemption is available.">
  <img src="/images/redemptions/code-voucher.png" alt="A reward voucher for Free Pastry on the Coffee Lovers Card, showing a Ready badge, the 8-digit code, the customer name and a Confirm Redemption button" />
</Frame>

#### One box, three kinds of code

Staff do not need to know where a code came from. Qtap checks three places for it: rewards the customer earned on a card, offers they claimed from your public page, and rewards they bought with a points balance. Whichever one matches, the voucher loads the same way.

<Frame caption="The same box loaded a claimed public offer. The strip above the voucher names the deal so staff know what to hand over.">
  <img src="/images/redemptions/code-offer.png" alt="A campaign voucher for 15% Off Any Drink loaded from a code, labelled Discount and Campaign Voucher, with the customer email redacted" />
</Frame>

Before the numeric format, Qtap issued codes that mixed letters, digits and a hyphen. Those still work until they expire, and the field takes them in upper or lower case.

<Note>
A code only resolves for the business you are signed in to. If a customer hands you a code from another Qtap merchant, the lookup reports **No reward found with this code**.
</Note>

#### When a code will not go through

If the code has already been used, the voucher loads with a **Redeemed** badge and the line "This reward has been redeemed" where the button would be, so staff cannot hand the same reward over twice.

<Frame caption="An already-used code loads with a Redeemed badge and no Confirm button.">
  <img src="/images/redemptions/code-redeemed.png" alt="A redeemed reward voucher showing a Redeemed badge and the message This reward has been redeemed" />
</Frame>

<Warning>
A voucher past its expiry date still shows a **Ready** badge and an active **Confirm Redemption** button, with a red **Expired** line underneath it. Confirming it fails and marks the reward expired, so check that line before you hand anything over.
</Warning>

### Look up the customer

Use this method when the customer doesn't have their code ready or you want to check what rewards they have before they ask.

<Frame caption="Look Up Customer: search by name, email, phone, or Qtap ID. Customer emails are hidden in this screenshot.">
  <img src="/images/redemptions/lookup-search.png" alt="The Look Up Customer search showing matching customers as results" />
</Frame>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Click Look Up Customer">
    On the Redeem tab, click **Look Up Customer**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Search">
    Type at least 2 characters. You can search by first name, last name, email, phone number, or Qtap ID. Results appear as you type.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select the customer">
    Click their row. Their pending rewards load automatically below their profile card.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Redeem a reward">
    Find the reward and click **Redeem**. A dialog titled Confirm Redemption shows the reward name and the customer's name. Click **Confirm Redemption** in the dialog to process it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
Qtap ID (the short code on every customer profile: the letter Q followed by six digits, like Q102812) is the most precise search. Ask returning customers to save it to their phone; it's on their profile card in the app.
</Tip>

## Points-based programs

If your organization runs on a points program rather than stamp cards, the Look Up Customer method shows an additional section after you select a customer.

When the customer has enough points to claim a reward, you'll see their current balance and a list of rewards they're eligible for. Each reward shows the points it costs. Click **Redeem**, then confirm. Qtap deducts the points and records the redemption.

<Frame caption="On a points program, the customer's balance and the rewards they can afford appear once you select them.">
  <img src="/images/redemptions/member-points.png" alt="A selected customer's points balance and the list of rewards they can redeem" />
</Frame>

The confirmation dialog shows the cost and the customer's new balance before you commit, so you can read it back to the customer if they ask.

<Frame caption="The confirmation shows the points cost and the customer's new balance before you commit.">
  <img src="/images/redemptions/confirm-points.png" alt="The Confirm Redemption dialog showing the reward, customer, points cost, and new balance" />
</Frame>

## Reward status

A reward processes only when its status is **available** or **pending redemption**. The status appears on the reward card.

| Status | What it means |
|---|---|
| Available | Ready to redeem |
| Pending redemption | Customer tapped Redeem in the app and is waiting for staff to confirm |
| Redeemed | Already processed, can't be used again |
| Expired | Past the expiry date set when the reward was created |

<Warning>
Redemptions can't be reversed from the dashboard. If you confirm a redemption by mistake, contact support@qtap.qa and the team can help correct it.
</Warning>

## Redemption history

The **History** tab shows the last 50 redemptions across your organization. Each row shows:

- Customer name
- Reward description
- Stamp card or program the reward came from
- Date and time it was processed
- Which staff member confirmed it

Use this when a customer questions whether their reward was already redeemed, or to track which staff member processed a specific transaction.

<Note>
One kind of redemption is missing from this tab. When a claimed offer pays out bonus stamps or bonus points, Qtap books it as earning rather than as a redemption, so it lands in the customer's activity instead of here. Offers that hand over a discount, a free item or a buy-one-get-one do appear.
</Note>
